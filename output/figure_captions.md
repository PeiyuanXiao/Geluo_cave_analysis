**Figure 1. Factor analysis of mixed data (FAMD) of complete flakes.**
FAMD of complete flakes from Geluo Cave (*n* = 116), based on eight active
attributes: six quantitative (length, width, thickness, log mass, dorsal scar
count, and interior platform angle [IPA]) and two categorical (platform type and
dorsal scar pattern). The first two dimensions account for 21.3% and 13.2% of the
total inertia. (a) Individual flake scores on Dimensions 1–2, coloured by
stratigraphic layer (Layer 2, pink; Layer 3, blue), with convex hulls, group
centroids (large open circles), and spokes joining each flake to its centroid;
layer was projected as a supplementary (illustrative) variable. The two layers
overlap almost entirely, and a PERMANOVA on Gower distances detects no significant
difference in flake attributes between them (*P* = 0.29, 9,999 permutations).
(b) Correlation circle of the six quantitative variables, showing their loadings on
Dimensions 1–2. (c) Coordinates of the categories of the two categorical variables
(platform type, green circles; dorsal scar pattern, purple triangles). IPA was set
to 0° for linear and punctiform platforms, and flakes lacking a determinable dorsal
scar pattern were grouped as "Indeterminate".

**Figure 2. Technological attributes of complete flakes by Toth reduction type.**
Attributes of complete flakes grouped by Toth flake type (I–VI) and bipolar-on-anvil
flakes (BOA). (a–c) Boxplots of (a) elongation (length/width), (b) interior platform
angle (IPA, degrees), and (c) dorsal scar count. Boxes show the median and
interquartile range, whiskers extend to 1.5× the interquartile range, coloured
points are individual flakes (jittered), and the black filled circle marks the mean.
IPA (b) is shown only for types I–VI, as bipolar-on-anvil flakes lack a measurable
striking platform. (d–f) Stacked bar charts of flake counts per type: bar height is
the total number of flakes, and the coloured segments give the relative composition
of (d) cortex location, (e) platform type, and (f) dorsal scar pattern. Types I–VI
follow the Toth flake-type scheme; BOA denotes bipolar-on-anvil flaking, treated as
a distinct reduction technology.

**Figure 3. Size distributions of the main artifact classes.**
Metric size of the four principal artifact classes — cores, percussion flakes,
bipolar-on-anvil flakes, and retouched flakes. Horizontal boxplots show (top left)
length, (top right) width, (bottom left) thickness (all in mm), and (bottom right)
mass (g, log₁₀ scale). Boxes show the median and interquartile range, whiskers
extend to 1.5× the interquartile range, coloured points are individual artifacts
(jittered), and the black filled circle marks the mean. Mass is plotted on a log₁₀
scale because a small number of large cores would otherwise compress the flake
distributions.
